12D — Signal Learning & Trade Lifecycle Intelligence

Separating the entry decision from position management, and measuring what the existing 12C signal actually does.
12D-signal-learning-v1 2,559 signal episodes 39 sessions · NIFTY + SENSEX Observation only — no thresholds changed No ML 2026-09-24
Read this first This is research on a loss-making sample. Nothing here demonstrates an edge. Separating entry from position management changed the shape of the results without changing their sign. All figures are pre-cost beyond the bid-ask spread — no brokerage, STT or slippage is modelled.

1 · The architectural change

Your premise was correct, and it was written down in the existing simulator's own docstring:

* WAIT → CLOSE at that minute's BID (reason WAIT).

That conflates two different questions. WAIT means "I would not open a position now" — it does not mean "get out of the one you have."

Before

One engine

Entry decision drives everything. A WAIT closes the position at that minute's bid, whatever the position itself is doing. Hold times 1–3 minutes.

After (12D)

Two engines

The position manager never reads the entry decision. It escalates HOLD → CAUTION → PREPARE_EXIT → EXIT with confirmation across minutes, and clean minutes step it back down. Average hold 6.4 minutes.

Exit categories are kept strictly apart: STOP_LOSS and DATA_RISK are hard and immediate; POSITION_SIGNAL requires consecutive adverse minutes and a broken premium trend. A single weak minute can only reach CAUTION.

A test asserts that identical evidence produces an identical verdict whether the entry engine says WAIT, BUY CE, BUY PE or nothing at all. The existing simulator is untouched, so both run on the same signals and can be compared.

4 · The diagnostic matrix, and why I distrust it

Exit goodExit bad
Good entry1861,987
Bad entry6366

Direction correct in 2,311 of 2,559 (90.3%).

Read this matrix with suspicion — I would not act on it. My exit_ok test demands capturing ≥40% of MFE. My entry_timing_ok test only asks that the first minute wasn't −3%. A lenient entry test next to a strict exit test mechanically produces "GOOD_ENTRY / EXIT_BAD". And direction_correct only needs MFE ≥ 0.5%, which nearly any option touches at some point. The matrix is substantially an artifact of my own definitions.

6 · Limitations

  • 39 sessions is not a regime. Five groups fall below N=20 and are marked INSUFFICIENT SAMPLE.
  • Pre-cost beyond the spread — no brokerage, STT or slippage.
  • Entry assumes a fill at the ASK, which flatters a real fill.
  • All labels come from provisional boundaries chosen a priori, never fitted to outcomes.

8 · Recommended next step

No threshold change is recommended, and none was made. Two hypotheses for a future milestone:

H1 — trade frequency, not exit timing, is the dominant cost. The spread is ₹0.42/unit against a −₹0.62/unit result. If that holds, the lever is taking far fewer trades, not managing them better. This is testable without touching a single 12C threshold.

H2 — ENTRY_TIMING and MOMENTUM_STATE as built have no predictive content and should be rebuilt or dropped rather than tuned.
