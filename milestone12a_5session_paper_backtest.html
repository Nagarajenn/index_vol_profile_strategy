Conclusion

A. What the ₹15,000 account would have done: on the only 2 replayable sessions, with real exchange lots it took 1 trade (a NIFTY PE on 09-18) and ended at ₹14,990.25. Every other gate-passing candidate breached the ₹300-per-trade cap with one 65-unit NIFTY lot. With the config's unit sizing it would have taken 6 trades and ended at ₹14,815.35.
B. Trades: 1 (lots) / 6 (units). C. Net P&L: ₹-9.75 / ₹-184.65 (net of spread, before fees). D. Ending balance: ₹14,990.25 / ₹14,815.35. E. Max drawdown: ₹9.75 / ₹184.65.
F. Why: most trades were detected early by the futures measure (5 of 6 in the unit view), but after the realistic fill 10 s later the option premium did not follow through; the active exits (event invalidation, futures/option divergence, momentum failure) closed every trade within 5–60 s at small losses. The bid/ask spread (₹75.69 in the unit view) was a large share of the loss. No trade reached the target or the stop; losses were 3–25% of the planned risk.
G. Missed opportunities: 8 (median +0.30% of premium) — small, and mostly weak near-miss events.
H. Status: no change. Two sessions and 1–6 trades are far too few to judge the strategy, and the result is indistinguishable from the random baseline. 12A remains RESEARCH / SHADOW ONLY; the paper-trading gate is unchanged. Separately, this replay shows that the current ₹300 per-trade cap and 5% stop are incompatible with one NIFTY lot at typical 12A premiums; that is a sizing-policy question to review at the gate, not something changed here.
