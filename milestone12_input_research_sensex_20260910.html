1. Executive summary

The central finding overturns the framing of the question. The +273 points did not happen between 15:15 and 15:30 in any way our underlying feed recorded. From 15:15 to 15:28 the SENSEX 1-minute series is completely frozen — fourteen consecutive candles with open = high = low = close = 74,629.50, range 0.00, and volume stuck at the identical value 48,157 carried over from 15:14. The entire +273 appears in our database as one single bar at 15:29. It is not a 15-minute move in our data; it is a one-minute step.
But the move itself was real, and the option chain proves it — and dates it. The option feed kept updating while the index feed was stale. Reconstructing the underlying from put–call parity on mid-quotes across 11 strikes (valid at DTE = 0, where S ≈ K + CE − PE) gives an internally consistent estimate (interquartile spread of only 1–9 points across strikes). It shows the underlying at 74,556 at 15:19, 74,789 at 15:20 and 74,990 at 15:21 — while our index feed still read 74,629.50. Option volume surged in the same minutes, from a ~6–13 M/min baseline to +33.9 M (CE) and +25.0 M (PE) at 15:21. At 15:29 the index feed caught up to 74,902.59 and parity read 74,906. From 15:30 the two agree to within 0.1 point.
So the honest answer is a combination — option H — with a specific decomposition: a genuine underlying repricing occurred in roughly a two-minute window around 15:20–15:21 (option-implied, INFERRED), our index feed failed to record it (OBSERVED), and it therefore surfaces in our data as a closing-print artifact at 15:29 (OBSERVED). Expiry mechanics are visible and material — every option converged to intrinsic value against 74,902.59 — but we cannot establish what caused the underlying to move. There is no order-flow, no dealer positioning, and no trustworthy underlying volume after 15:14.
A second finding that matters more for Milestone 12 than the spike does. The 14:59 note "Bearish price but Put OI concentration is increasing" is arithmetically true but mechanically misleading. Between 14:30 and 14:59, put open interest fell at every single strike in ATM±5 (−145 K to −1.43 M). PCR rose only because call OI fell faster. Nothing was accumulating. A ratio increase driven by a collapsing denominator was reported in language that implies a growing numerator — and that is a systematic wording defect, not a one-day accident.

What does the 15:30 value actually represent?

The audit brief said not to assume — so this was tested against ingestion semantics and against other days.

Ingestion semantics. raw_candles is written by db.writer.insert_raw_candles from Dhan's /charts/intraday response; the pipeline performs no synthesis, no forward-fill and no interpolation. Therefore the repeated bars came from the broker feed itself — Dhan returned the last known value repeatedly. Our code did not manufacture them.

Cross-day test. The pattern is systematic, not a one-off:

Conclusion on the 15:30 value. It is not a normal traded 1-minute close and not a special checkpoint in our code. It is the settlement / closing value arriving as a single late feed refresh, after roughly 14 minutes during which the index feed published nothing new. Every trading day in the sample shows the same shape, with steps ranging from −401 to +273 — today's is simply one of the larger ones. Any research that treats 15:15→15:30 as an observed intraday path is measuring a feed artifact. NIFTY additionally receives a one-off 119,454,668 volume print at its jump minute; SENSEX receives none, so SENSEX's step carries no volume information whatsoever.

19. Direct answer: why +273?

Evidence level: 2 — MODERATE EVIDENCE for what and when. Level 4 — CANNOT DETERMINE for why.

Of the offered options the answer is H (a combination), specifically C + D + I: repositioning/unwinding in the 15:15-15:30 window, expiry mechanics, and a session-data effect in how it reaches our database. It is not A (nothing before 15:00 anticipated it) and not B (the 15:00-15:15 transition was bearish and compressive).

PRIMARY OBSERVATION. The SENSEX index feed published nothing new for fourteen consecutive minutes (15:15-15:28): identical OHLC and a frozen volume field. The +273 enters our data as one bar at 15:29. The same shape occurs on every day sampled, with steps from −401 to +273. OBSERVED

SECONDARY OBSERVATION. The option chain, which never froze, repriced the underlying from ~74,556 to ~74,990 between 15:19 and 15:21 — eleven strikes agreeing to within a 7-point IQR — and converged exactly to 74,902.59 intrinsic by 15:29. A real move occurred, roughly nine minutes before our index feed showed it, and it was abrupt (two minutes) rather than gradual. INFERRED

THIRD OBSERVATION. Option volume tripled-to-septupled in exactly those minutes (+6-13 M/min baseline → +33.9 M CE / +25.0 M PE at 15:21), and CE open interest fell 16.0 M across the window. Real trading and real unwinding accompanied the repricing. OBSERVED

WHAT WE CANNOT PROVE. We cannot say what caused the underlying to move. There is no order-flow data, no trustworthy underlying volume after 15:14, no dealer positioning, and no way to separate closing-auction mechanics from directional buying. Specifically we cannot claim: that gamma or dealer hedging drove it (dealer exposure is not inferable from this data, and no dealer position is invented here); that "post-market buy/sell volume" caused it (§14); that option activity predicted the move (the lead is a feed-staleness artifact, §10); or that any 14:59-observable precursor pointed to it. The nearest thing to a precursor found anywhere in the session is the 15:12 volume spike of 259,837 — and that is suggestive at best, eight minutes ahead, on a single minute.

23. Final conclusion

The question "why did SENSEX move +273 between 15:15 and 15:30?" contains a false premise, and correcting it is the most valuable result of this exercise. In our data, no such 15-minute move exists. There is a fourteen-minute hole where the index feed published nothing, followed by a single settlement print. The level 74,902.59 is genuine — the entire option chain converges to it to within 0.1 point, independently. But the path is unrecorded, and the option market places the actual repricing in a two-minute window around 15:20-15:21, roughly nine minutes before our feed showed it.

What today establishes for Milestone 12:

  1. A stale-feed / closing-print classifier is the single highest-priority feature. Without it, the platform will keep computing structure, volume and transition statistics over straight lines that never traded — on every symbol, on every day.
  2. Close-to-close outcome and observed intraday transition must be reported as two different things. Today they disagree by 261 points and by direction.
  3. Put-call parity is a free, already-captured second opinion on the underlying that works precisely when the primary feed fails.
  4. The "Put OI concentration is increasing" wording defect is real, systematic, and will recur. It is arguably more consequential than the spike itself, because it will mislead on ordinary days too.
  5. Some things remain unknowable with this data — dealer gamma, post-15:15 underlying volume, and the actual cause of the move. Milestone 12 should be built to say so explicitly rather than to fill the gap.
Nothing here justifies changing the trading strategy, and no such change is recommended. This is one session, one symbol. The 14:59 decision itself was sound on the information available — it declined a directional call it could not support, and its expected-move band correctly bracketed everything that was actually observable. No threshold was tuned, no rule was created, and none of these features were added to the agent.
