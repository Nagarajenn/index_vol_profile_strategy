3 · Did the option respond?

Elasticity is the measured |option %| ÷ |underlying %|. For an ATM option it is normally 90–200 simply because the premium is a small number, so on its own it says little.

Response efficiency divides that by the elasticity delta implies (|delta| × spot ÷ premium). 1.0 means the premium moved exactly as first-order theory predicts. This is the number that matters.

9 · The fifteen questions

1. When the underlying falls, how often does PE actually rise?At the 10m horizon, of 19 measurable PE signals 63.2% had the underlying fall and the option profitable.
2. When the underlying falls, how often does PE fail to rise?5.3% of measurable PE signals at 10m: the underlying moved down but the option still lost.
3. When PE fails despite the underlying falling, what explains it?Attributed reasons for losing PE trades, most common first: iv headwind (4), underlying did not continue (3), late entry (2).
4. How much of today's PE loss comes from spread?Mean execution friction on PE was 0.34/unit against a mean executable result of 2.49/unit. 2 of 17 losing PE trades (11.8%) would have been positive mid-to-mid, so the spread alone turned them negative.
5. How much comes from weak option response?Median response efficiency across horizons was 1.12 (1.0 = exactly what delta implies), and only 0 losing PE trades were attributed to weak response or strike distance. Option response is not a material cause today.
6. How much comes from IV contraction?4 losing PE trades were attributed to an IV headwind. Mean IV change over the hold on PE was -0.73%.
7. How much comes from late entry?2 losing PE trades were attributed to late entry. On average 50% of the underlying movement around a PE signal happened in the 5 minutes before it.
8. How much comes from the underlying reversing after entry?2 losing PE trades reversed after going the right way first, and 3 never went the right way at any measured horizon.
9. Does strike distance matter?By band: atm N=29, mean 1.78/unit; itm N=10, mean -2.75/unit (small N). Not separable at today's sample size.
10. Does delta matter?By |delta|: 0.4-0.6 N=39, mean 0.62/unit.
11. Does time-of-day matter?Best band 09:15-10:00 (N=5, mean 13.32/unit), worst 11:00-12:00 (N=16, mean -5.08/unit). Every band is below the minimum sample today — not interpretable on one session.
12. Are repeated signals causing overtrading?6.2 signals per hour, median gap 9 minutes, 1 of 37 gaps under 3 minutes. Same-direction runs average 2.29 signals (max 7), so one move does produce several entries.
13. Are signal flips causing unnecessary churn?15 direction flips; 100% were confirmed by the underlying actually moving against the old side, median gap 9 minutes. Every flip followed a real move in the underlying, so the churn is the market's, not the engine's.
14. Is the current &quot;direction correct&quot; metric misleading?Yes. 12D's MFE ≥ 0.5% proxy reported 90.3% across history. Measured from the underlying itself, today's 10-minute figure is 50.0% on 30 measurable signals, with 9 unmeasurable because spot barely moved. The old number was measuring the option ticking up, not the market being read correctly.
15. What is the largest measurable source of today's losses?By count, iv headwind (6 trades). But the structural finding is context: 24 of 27 PE signals fired while the underlying's own 10-minute state was RANGE, not TRENDING DOWN. The day's decline was a slow drift, so at the scale the signal operates on there was usually no trend to capture — and the round trip still cost 0.33/unit.
