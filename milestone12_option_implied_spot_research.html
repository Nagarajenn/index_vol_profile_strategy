Milestone 12 Research Extension
Option-implied spot as a leading market signal — read-only

Focus: SENSEX 2026-09-10 · Comparison set: every SENSEX and NIFTY day with option data (2026-07-15 → 2026-09-11) · Frozen experiment: 11d-paper-v1 / 9c7c362d7e0c6a14, untouched.

1. Executive summary

First, a correction to the previous forensic report, because it changes the answer. milestone12_input_research_sensex_20260910.html described the flat index from 15:15 to 15:28 as a stale or frozen feed and called the option lead a "data-plumbing" artefact. That mechanism was wrong. Tested across every day in the database, the flat stretch appears on 60 of 60 symbol-days from 2026-08-03 onward and 0 of 26 before it. 2026-08-03 is exactly the project's CAS_EFFECTIVE_DATE. Before that date the index prints 12–13 different closes between 15:15 and 15:27; from that date, exactly one. The flat index is the Closing Auction Session, not a feed failure. No index is published from continuous trading while constituents are in auction, and the 15:28/15:29 step is the auction-determined close.
The option repricing is real and robust. An independent recalculation reproduces the original numbers to within ±1.6 points. The 15:20–15:28 spike survives every alternative method: median, mean, inverse-spread-weighted mean and last-traded price all agree within ~2 points. It is broad-based: at every key minute all 10 strikes in the band (ITM, ATM and OTM) imply the same level to within about ±6 points, on spreads of 0.2–1%.
The headline result is conditional, not general.
• On expiry days, the option-implied level during the auction anticipated the closing step with real skill. On SENSEX (n = 6), the 15:27 reading called the direction 6/6, correlated +0.97 with the actual step, and cut absolute error by 70% against a predict-no-change baseline. On NIFTY (n = 5): 4/5, correlation +0.90, error −71%.
• On non-expiry days it has no useful skill. The raw gap is dominated by futures-style carry and performs worse than predicting no change. De-biased, it improves error by only 2–9%.
• In continuous trading, options never lead the index. At every horizon from 1 to 5 minutes, the options→index correlation is ≈0 and direction agreement is ~50%, on pooled samples of over 1,000 minutes.
What this means. "Option-implied spot leads the index" is the wrong framing. The better-supported reading is narrower: on expiry day, during the closing auction, the option chain is a live market estimate of the auction close — because on that day those options literally settle to it. That estimate becomes reliable late (typically by 15:25–15:27, 1–4 minutes before the print). Earlier readings, around 15:20, were wrong-signed on 2 of 6 SENSEX expiry days, and gave a sustained false signal on NIFTY 2026-09-08. With n = 11 expiry days in total, this is a promising research lead, not a validated feature.

2. Existing implied-spot methodology

There is no existing implementation. A search of all Python, TypeScript and SQL for implied_spot, "implied spot", "put-call parity" and "parity" returns zero files. No derived table stores it. The table quoted in the brief came from an ad-hoc query run inline while writing the previous forensic report. Nothing in production computes it, so there was no existing derived table to trust or distrust.

What that ad-hoc query actually did, reconstructed exactly — including two flaws:

The spike survives every method. Across 15:20–15:28 the four estimators never differ by more than 2.9 points, against moves of 200–400 points. The recalculation uses a different strike band (74,200–75,100, anchored to 15:14 rather than a fixed 74,800) and still matches the original to within ±1.6 points at every minute. Last-traded prices instead of mids change nothing material. This is not an artefact of estimator choice.
Note the pre-auction drift in the gap. From 14:30 to 14:59 the gap sits near −15 to −30. From 15:00 to 15:14 — still continuous trading — it widens steadily to −86. So during the 15:00–15:14 window the chain was pricing a close below the index, and the eventual close was 273 points above it. The option estimate only turned right once the auction began. That matters for any claim that options "saw it coming".

5. IQR analysis

The hypothesis to test: large move + low cross-strike IQR = the whole chain is repricing, not a bad quote.

Window (2026-09-10, SENSEX)Typical IQRTypical |gap||gap| / IQR
14:30–14:59 (normal continuous trading)0.35 – 1.461 – 39often 10 – 90 (e.g. 14:53: −38.6 / 0.43 = −90)
15:00–15:14 (continuous, post-transition)0.74 – 3.9911 – 1024 – 60
15:20–15:28 (auction repricing)1.27 – 5.1425 – 4126 – 250
The hypothesis is only half right, and the half that fails matters.

What holds: a low IQR does rule out a single bad quote. If one strike were mispriced, the per-strike estimates would disagree and the IQR would blow out. At 15:21 the IQR was 5.14 on a 360-point gap, so this was not one bad print.

What fails: low IQR is not evidence of a strong signal, for a structural reason. Put-call parity is enforced by arbitrage: any internally consistent quoted chain will imply nearly the same underlying at every strike, whether or not that underlying is correct. Market makers quote every strike off one reference, so all strikes move together by construction. That is exactly why IQR is already under 1.5 in ordinary trading at 14:30. During the repricing, IQR actually rose (to 1.3–5.1), not fell. And gap/IQR is huge in ordinary minutes too (−90 at 14:53), so it cannot separate signal from normal conditions.

Conclusion: IQR is a validity check (is this estimate internally coherent?), not a strength measure (is this repricing meaningful?). Milestone 12 should use it only as a gate. The same pattern holds across days: auction-window IQR is higher than continuous-window IQR on most days in §9, with a single-minute peak of 17.1 on SENSEX 2026-08-27.

In continuous trading, option-implied spot does not lead the index — at any horizon, on either symbol, before or after CAS. opt→idx is between −0.08 and +0.11 everywhere, and direction agreement is 44–53%: a coin flip. The consistent non-zero relationship runs the other way, index → options at 1 minute (+0.34 to +0.49).
Do not read that 1-minute index lead as causal either. It is at least partly a sampling artefact. The live loop fetches the option chain about 18–24 seconds into the minute (visible in the log timestamps, e.g. 09:26:18 ... as_of=09:26:00) and stamps it with that minute's label. The candle for the same label keeps being refreshed until it closes at the end of the minute. So the "t" option snapshot is taken ~40 seconds before the "t" candle close, and naturally resembles the "t+1" candle more. Sub-minute lead/lag cannot be resolved with this sampling. What can be said: there is no evidence of options leading the index in continuous trading. The only place options appear to lead is the auction — where the index is not being published at all.

The vendor IV and Greeks are wrong during the auction, and this proves it. At strike 74,200 between 15:19 and 15:20, Dhan's CE IV rose +45 while PE IV fell −5. At the same strike and expiry, put-call parity forces CE and PE to share one implied volatility; they cannot move in opposite directions in a coherent market. By 15:21 the 74,700 PE shows IV = 0.0 while trading at 15.47. The explanation is that Dhan computes IV against the frozen index (74,629.50). A call worth 307.70 against a "spot" of 74,629.50 needs absurd volatility, and a put priced below its apparent intrinsic value has no solution, so it reads 0. Every vendor IV and Greek value from 15:15 to 15:28 on post-CAS days should be treated as invalid.
A related observation about the frozen 11D agent — flagged, not changed. The agent's delta floor is applied only at option selection at 14:59, before the auction, so it uses valid Greeks. But paper_trading/position_manager.py feeds the index price into classify_momentum and the ATR-based "underlying reversed against entry" exit. A 14:59 entry with the 19-minute maximum hold runs to 15:18, so for its last ~3 minutes those two checks read a flat auction index and cannot fire. Its bid-based stop, target and time exit are unaffected, because option quotes keep updating. Per the experiment freeze, this is recorded for Milestone 12 only.
Could 15:20 have been an early warning? Looking at this one day, it is tempting to say yes: the first +232 move arrived 9 minutes before the print. Across all expiry days, no. At 15:20 the gap had the wrong sign on SENSEX 2026-08-13 (−35 before a +219 step) and 2026-08-27 (+22 before a −249 step). On NIFTY 2026-09-08 it showed −49 before a −5 step, then stayed at −37 to −78 for six minutes before collapsing to +4. And on 2026-09-10 itself the 15:20 reading fully reversed a minute later (+360 → +25). 15:20 was right today in hindsight; it would not have been a reliable warning. See §9 for when the reading becomes reliable.

This contradicts the project's own CAS documentation. But the useful lead is short: skill climbs from 15:20 (SENSEX 4/6, corr +0.63) to 15:25 (6/6, +0.90) to 15:27 (6/6, +0.97), leaving roughly 1–2 minutes before the print. The raw estimate beats the de-biased one on expiry days, as theory predicts: at DTE 0 there is no carry to remove, and de-biasing throws away real information.
How often does it fail? On these 11 expiry days, at 15:27: SENSEX 0 of 6 wrong direction. NIFTY 1 of 5 — 2026-09-08, predicted +3.9 against an actual −5.0. Both are within noise of zero, so this is a near-flat day rather than a confident wrong call. (The de-biased estimate instead misses 2026-08-11, −3.0 against +21.5.) But at 15:20, 3 of 11 readings were wrong-signed or a sustained false signal (SENSEX 08-13, SENSEX 08-27, NIFTY 09-08). And magnitude errors at 15:27 are large on some days: SENSEX 08-27 predicted −375 vs −249 actual; 08-13 +116 vs +219. With n = 6 and n = 5, none of these rates is statistically established. A 6/6 hit rate has a 95% confidence interval of roughly 54–100%.

Why non-expiry days differ, mechanically. On a non-expiry day the options settle days later, not today, so parity gives the forward price, which sits above spot by the carry. The measured 15:05–15:14 baseline gap on SENSEX non-expiry days runs +25 to +272 points (its relationship to days-to-expiry was not tested here and is not claimed). The raw gap therefore mostly measures carry. After de-biasing, what remains correlates only ~+0.3 with the step and improves error by 2–9% — negligible. There is also a plausible economic reason: those options have no direct claim on today's auction close, so there is little incentive to price it precisely.

Classification against the brief's options: the gap is B — mostly expiry-specific as a signal. On non-expiry days it is A — normal carry and noise. It is not C (feed-specific): the flat index is the auction, which is identical on expiry and non-expiry days, yet the signal only works on expiry days. D (useful transition signal) is plausible for expiry days only, and E (insufficient sample) applies to that conclusion.

17. Final recommendation

Recommendation: keep this as a Milestone 12 research track, reframed — do not build it as a trading signal.
  1. Reframe the hypothesis. Not "option-implied spot leads the index". The supported claim is narrower: on expiry days, during the closing auction, the option chain is a live estimate of the auction close. Outside that window, there is no lead to exploit.
  2. Build market-state labelling first. Continuous / auction / outage / stuck is REQUIRED regardless of whether the option signal survives. It fixes a real misreading already present in the CAS documentation.
  3. Log, don't act. Compute and store the option-implied spot, gap, IQR and persistence for every future expiry day as a research series, then test it prospectively with thresholds fixed in advance. Roughly 20–30 more expiry days per symbol would turn a suggestive pattern into an assessable one.
  4. Prefer the 15:25–15:27 reading, with persistence, over the first large gap. The early reading is where the false signals live.
  5. Treat vendor IV/Greeks from 15:15 to 15:28 as invalid in any future analysis, and note the frozen 11D agent's index-based exit checks during that window for Milestone 12.
What this report does not recommend. No change to 11d-paper-v1, its configuration, its option selector, stop/target logic or exits. No threshold was tuned against 2026-09-10 or any other day. The signal was not added to paper trading. The correction to the earlier report and the contradiction with the CAS docstring are recorded, not fixed.
