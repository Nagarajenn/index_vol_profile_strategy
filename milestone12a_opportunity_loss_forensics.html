10. The five factual questions

  1. Where does 12A lose the most opportunities? By count, most candidates are stopped by the time/mode/expiry window (31 of 60, mostly Mode B and pre-15:00) and by confirmation (21, dominated by WEAK_MOMENTUM: 38 of all candidates are near-miss events below the 95th-percentile threshold). Inside the tradeable Mode A window (29 candidates) confirmation removes the most (21), and then the exchange-lot risk gate removes 6 of the 7 survivors. Among counterfactually profitable rejections the first gate was confirmation 7, window 6, risk 1.
  2. Is the risk gate preventing valid trades because of exchange lot size? It blocks every gate-passing NIFTY candidate but one (6 blocked). One 65-unit lot at the selected premiums needs ₹6,672–9,357 of capital and risks ₹334–468 at the 5% stop, so both caps bind. Raising either cap alone unlocks nothing; only raising both does (₹400 / ₹7500: 3 trades, net ₹-133.25; ₹400 / ₹10000: 5 trades, net ₹-162.50; ₹500 / ₹7500: 3 trades, net ₹-133.25; ₹500 / ₹10000: 6 trades, net ₹-243.75). The blocked candidates' own one-lot counterfactual sum is ₹-307.95. The gate is structurally lot-bound, but in this sample the trades it blocked would mostly have lost.
  3. Is the entry delay consuming too much of the move? Not measurably in this sample. Across all 60 candidates the median active-exit result is similar at 0, 1, 2 and 3 bars (0: -0.41%), (1: -0.39%), (2: -0.31%), (3: -0.28%). The move is mostly consumed before detection: momentum events are only recognised after their 30-second move, and the detection bar is often the local extreme, so a 0-bar fill is not better.
  4. Is option-response confirmation filtering useful opportunities? Rarely on its own: only 1 candidate was blocked by it alone. The flagged group was not worse than the rest (32.1% vs 15.6% counterfactually profitable), so there is no evidence here that it filters bad trades. Its timing is also mismatched: 11 of 28 flagged candidates had a ≥1% response that faded before the detection bar, and 6 responded only afterwards. On this sample the 30-second response measure looks disconnected from the scalp outcome rather than protective, but the sample is too small to separate the four hypotheses.
  5. Are the missed profitable opportunities meaningful enough to justify another research iteration? As captured by the current active exit, mostly not: 8 missed, 6 of them under +1%, 1 at 1–2%, 1 above 5% (outside the trading window). However, the option moved: 33 rejected candidates saw an MFE of 2% or more within 300 s; for them the median time-to-MFE was 200 s (67% peaked after the 120-second hold), the median MAE on the way was -2.91%, and the active exit's median result was -0.26%. The opportunity is present in the market but is not capturable by the current entry and exit design. That is a research question, not a tuning result.

WHERE WE ARE LOSING THE OPPORTUNITY

  • Before detection. Events are recognised only after their 30-second (or 180-second) move; the median event-start-to-detection time is 35 s and much of the displacement is already spent.
  • At confirmation. WEAK_MOMENTUM near-misses are the largest single rejection reason (38 of 60); they include 7 counterfactually profitable candidates, almost all small.
  • At risk sizing with real lots. 6 of the 7 gate-passing NIFTY candidates are blocked because one lot breaches both the ₹300 and ₹6,000 caps. SENSEX produced no gate-passing candidate.
  • At the exit. 33 of the 59 rejected candidates' options moved 2%+ within 5 minutes, but typically late (median 200 s, 67% after the 120-second hold) and after a -2.91% median adverse move; the active exit (as designed) realises a median -0.26% on them.
  • Not at the entry delay (0–3 bars give similar results) and not mainly at option response alone (the sole blocker for 1 candidate).
  • All of this is descriptive: 2 days, 60 candidates, 1–6 trades. No parameter change is recommended or made.
