Milestone 12B — Option Chain Risk & Closing-State Intelligence
Version 12B-option-risk-v1 · config hash 1eadd228220900aa · run 12b-20260920T165452 · advisory / research only · 11D (hash 9c7c362d7e0c6a14) and 12A (hash 994667c6d552111e) unchanged

Read this first. This milestone adds a way to see the option chain while the index print is stale, and an advisory risk monitor. It does not place, modify or cancel orders, does not open or close paper positions, and does not change any 11D or 12A decision. 11D has no historical paper positions (paper_positions is empty), so the position-risk study uses clearly labelled hypothetical reference positions (ATM BUY CE and ATM BUY PE). Every threshold is a RESEARCH DEFAULT. No result below is a trading edge.

Answers to the 15 questions

1. Does option-chain data actually exist from 15:15–15:30?

Yes, for 15:15–15:29. On 76 of 87 symbol-days with any option data (2026-07-17 → 2026-09-18) there is a snapshot every minute from 15:15 to 15:29. The 15:30 minute is missing on 74 symbol-days, because the live loop's last capture is 15:29. After a gap, snapshots resume at about 15:36–15:39. The CE and PE legs, bid/ask, OI, volume and IV fields are all present in the raw payload.

2. How many minutes are available?

1114 of 1216 expected window minutes (15:15–15:30 inclusive).

3. How many missing minutes?

102. Almost all of them are the 15:30 minute (74). The 15:15–15:28 minutes are missing on only 2 symbol-days: 2026-07-17, the first capture day, which has a single snapshot at 15:29. Another 11 symbol-days have no snapshot after 15:15 at all (listed in the audit). The capture process was not changed: the 15:30 gap comes from the live-loop session boundary, which is protected scheduling, not a capture defect.

4. How often is the underlying stale?

On 62 of 78 symbol-days the index print stopped updating after 15:15. The exceptions are the July days, where the index kept printing. In minute terms, 884 of 1114 window minutes with a snapshot (79%) were STALE or CLOSING_STATE_UNCERTAIN. The typical pattern is a flat 15:15 print that persists until a new value appears near 15:29–15:30. We describe this; we do not name a mechanism.

5. How often are options still changing while the underlying is stale?

884 of 884 stale-underlying minutes (100%) had option activity. Activity here means at least 4 of the ATM±5 legs' mids changed from the previous minute.

6. Does CE/PE movement precede the next reliable underlying print?

Directionally, somewhat — but not beyond the base rate. Over 62 stale symbol-days, the CE-minus-PE premium change at 5 minutes agreed with the next print's direction 75.4% of the time (n=57). However, 66.1% of next prints were UP in this sample. Always guessing the majority direction would have scored 64.9% on the same days, and the binomial p against that baseline is 0.061. Option movement preceded the print in the same direction more often than not, but this sample cannot separate that from the market's drift.

7. Does option pressure provide useful directional information during the stale period?

Not demonstrated. The composite OPTION_DIRECTIONAL_PRESSURE hit 68.4% at 5 minutes, 43.9% at 10 minutes and 68.8% at about 15 minutes. That is unstable, and at best level with the majority baseline. The implied-spot gap at ~15 minutes was associated with the print's size (Spearman ρ 0.56, p 0.002, n=29), and its sign agreed 85.2% of the time (majority baseline 77.8%, p 0.251). That is an association in a small sample, not validated directional information.

8. Does option-chain evidence give an earlier warning of adverse movement for an existing paper position?

Not on this evidence. There are no real paper positions to study. On 296 hypothetical reference positions, every position received at least one PREPARE_EXIT / BREAK_EXIT (296 of 296), so the warning is not selective. Over the next 5 minutes, BREAK_EXIT minutes were followed by a median -0.33% option move against -0.81% after HOLD minutes. −1% came before +1% in 46.9% of BREAK_EXIT cases against 57.7% of HOLD cases. Warnings were not followed by worse outcomes than HOLD; if anything the opposite, which is consistent with short-term mean reversion.

9. Which variables appear useful descriptively?

(a) The underlying state itself: it is explicit and reliable, and a frozen print is never shown as live. (b) Option activity while the underlying is stale: the option chain keeps changing. (c) Option-implied spot (parity) as a description of where the option market sits relative to the frozen print. It tracks the live index within a few points on live days, and its gap was associated with the size of the next print. (d) CE vs PE relative premium change as a readable summary. All four are descriptive; none is a validated signal.

10. Which variables are unavailable or unreliable?

The index print itself after ~15:15 (stale on most days since August). The 15:30 option snapshot (not captured). Index volume (the 1-min index candle volume does not change once the print freezes). Greeks and IV for deep-ITM legs. The composite directional pressure, which flips between horizons. The dated news feed (it ended 2026-08-19, so it is not used).

11. How often do Greeks become invalid?

8.9% of ATM±5 leg-minutes had zero or degenerate IV/Greeks. They are concentrated in ITM puts (24.9%) and ITM calls (10.7%). They are marked INVALID, never repaired or replaced by zero.

12. How often does the bid/ask spread expand?

ATM CE/PE spread was at least 1.5× its 15:15 value in 22.6% of window leg-minutes, and at least 3.0% wide in 2.2%.

13. How much warning time exists before adverse movement?

Of the reference positions that fell 5% from entry (176), 134 had a warning first, with a median lead of 4.0 minutes. But all 120 positions that never fell 5% were also warned. The lead time is therefore not meaningful: a warning that fires on every position gives no information about which ones will fall.

14. Does this justify adding an OPTION RISK BRAKE to the future scalping decision architecture?

Not yet. The visibility part (underlying state, option continuation, implied spot, explicit reasons) is justified as a display and a diagnostic. A brake that changes decisions is not: the current evidence ladder saturates on ordinary option noise, and warned minutes were not followed by worse outcomes. A future brake would need thresholds scaled to each option's own recent volatility, fixed in advance, and tested out of sample on real (paper) positions.

15. Does the evidence justify automatic exits?

No. Keep the monitor advisory. The 11D exit engine remains authoritative.

3. Most important question — does the option chain tell us the direction of the next reliable print?

For each stale symbol-day (62): the frozen value is the last LIVE print; the next reliable print is the first later 1-minute candle close that differs from it (sources: {'FIRST_CHANGED_1MIN_CANDLE': 62}). Option signals are taken 5, 10 and ~15 minutes after the stale start. They must be strictly before the print is observable; otherwise they are excluded, which is why the 15-minute n is smaller. Prints within ±{'NIFTY': 5.0, 'SENSEX': 15.0} pts are treated as flat and excluded. The majority baseline is ‘always guess the direction most prints took on these same days’.

The change was made on the independence argument and the saturation count, not on the forward-outcome tables. The earlier version's outcome summary is kept in data/cache/option_risk_12b/v0_flat_count_summary.json. Both versions reach the same conclusion: the warning is not selective. No threshold was tuned to outcomes.
Why it saturates: in the last 30 minutes an ATM option's premium routinely moves more than 1% in 2 minutes on ordinary index noise, and theta decay produces falling straddles every minute. Fixed-percentage research defaults therefore fire on noise. A credible brake would need thresholds expressed relative to each contract's own recent minute-return distribution, fixed in advance, and tested on real paper positions out of sample.

8. Paper Trading Command Center — new section (UI description)

A new section, “15:15–15:30 OPTION RISK & CLOSING STATE”, was added to the existing Paper Trading page. It reads the new read-only endpoint GET /api/v1/option-risk-12b/{symbol}/closing-state?session_date= and has a NIFTY/SENSEX toggle and a date picker (default: today, or the latest captured session). The same continuation view is also placed under the live CAS tracker on the Market Transition page. Blocks:

  • Underlying status: a per-minute strip from 15:00 to 15:30, visually split into 15:00–15:15 ACTUAL UNDERLYING and 15:15–15:30 CLOSING / STALE UNDERLYING + OPTION CHAIN CONTINUATION. It shows LIVE / STALE / CLOSING_STATE_UNCERTAIN / MISSING / NEW PRINT, plus the last reliable value, its time, the current time and the age. A frozen value is never shown as live.
  • Option chain pressure: minute rows with CE, PE, PCR, CE/PE OI Δ, CE/PE volume Δ, CE/PE IV, straddle, the pressure label and the risk state, with CE/PE movement colour-coded.
  • Option movement charts: (1) CE % and PE % premium change since 15:00 plus the ATM straddle, (2) CE/PE intraday OI change, (3) CE/PE volume per minute. These are three separate charts.
  • Position risk: when a paper position exists, it shows the position, current option price, POSITION_SUPPORT, RISK, RISK_ACTION (HOLD / CAUTION / PREPARE_EXIT / BREAK_EXIT) and the explicit reasons, each marked ADVISORY. Otherwise it reads MARKET OPTION STATE.
  • Closing state: underlying state, options activity, option-derived state (CE / PE / NEUTRAL / MIXED), implied spot, gap, confidence (LOW / MODERATE / UNKNOWN, never HIGH), and the data-quality caveat, which is always shown.

The endpoint is GET-only. It reads option_chain_raw, raw_candles and paper_positions, and writes nothing. No button in the section can change a position.
