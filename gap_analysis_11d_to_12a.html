11D → 12A Gap Analysis
Where 11d-paper-v1 rejected, what happened next, and what 12A-scalp-v1 would need

Prepared: 2026-09-18 · Scope: read-only research — no strategy, configuration, pipeline or database changes · Control: 11d-paper-v1 / 9c7c362d7e0c6a14, unchanged

The short answer.
  1. The premise needs one correction. 11D did not reject only on conflict. Of the 10 experiment decisions: 5 were CONFLICTING_SIGNALS, 2 were direction-related (LOW_CONFIDENCE, NO_CLEAR_DIRECTION), 2 were EXPECTED_MOVE_TOO_SMALL and 1 was PRICE_AT_MAJOR_SUPPORT.
  2. The bigger gap is architectural, not a threshold. 11D makes one decision per symbol per day, at 14:59, and then stops looking. Every short-lived event in 15:00–15:30 is invisible to it by design.
  3. Short-lived opportunities did exist. Judged in hindsight, one ATM leg bought at 15:00 at the ask gave ≥+3% within 3 minutes with a worst dip no lower than −2% on 11 of 14 symbol-days. That is a hindsight label, not a claim of predictability.
  4. A naive event detector is not an edge. On the 4 days with 5-second data, entering on large 30-second futures displacements did no better than random entry (60-second median best move +1.04% versus +1.21% for random). So confirmation is essential, which matches your spec.
  5. There is one demonstrably dangerous condition. All five losses of 27% or worse (up to −93% within 3 minutes) happened on expiry-day contracts, four of them from 15:15 onward. This is the one case where a hard veto is justified by evidence.
  6. Validation data is thin. 34 days of 1-minute option chains support 1-minute research now. 5-second rules have only 4 days (36 events), which is not enough for any chronological split.

Three structural observations about 11D itself. These are recorded for the control and must not be changed.
  • A risk factor that is always true. "Support and resistance both inside 0.5×ATR" appears in 14 of 14 decisions. The daily-ATR band (about 85 points on NIFTY and 300–340 on SENSEX) is wider than typical support/resistance spacing, so this factor carries no information.
  • The trend label and the transition forecast often disagree by construction. The trend label describes the day so far (it is a lagging read); the transition forecast describes 15:00–15:15. On 09-11 (Strong Bullish, forecast DOWN 70%) and 09-15 SENSEX they point opposite ways, which guarantees CONFLICTING_SIGNALS.
  • An inconsistent probability field. probability_no_move is 1.0 on rows where p_up + p_down = 1. It is a display/semantics inconsistency and did not drive any decision.

What this table does and does not show.
  • Liquidity was never the blocker. ATM spreads at 15:00 were 0.03–0.35% on every contract. SPREAD_TOO_WIDE would not have fired at 15:00 on any day.
  • The trend label at 14:59 was a coin flip for a 3-minute scalp. Following it gave a clean leg on 09-09, 09-10, 09-16 NIFTY and 09-17 SENSEX, but was wrong or flat on 09-11, 09-15 and 09-18. A scalp strategy cannot take its direction from the day's structure.
  • 09-18 SENSEX fell 134 points in 15 minutes, but only as a slow grind. Nothing large happened in any 3-minute or 30-second window. A 30-second displacement detector misses this type of move completely (see §3).

3. Event-level evidence (5-second data, 09-15 to 09-18)

Detector used for this diagnosis (not a proposed 12A rule): the 30-second futures move exceeds the 95th percentile of the same symbol's 30-second moves on the other HR days (leave-one-day-out, so there is no same-day leakage). Consecutive triggers within 60 seconds count as one event, so the 5-second observations are not treated as independent. Entry is the direction-matched ATM leg at the ask on the next 5-second bar; exits are measured on the bid. That gives 36 events, compared with 320 random entries (random time, random CE/PE) in the same windows.

Finding: displacement alone is not tradeable. Event entries are statistically indistinguishable from random entries: a similar median best move, a similar median dip, and a negative median fixed-time exit at most horizons. Across all 36 events the median time to the best move (180-second horizon) was 60 s and to the worst dip 95 s. So without an active exit, the typical scalp gives back its gain before the horizon ends. This supports two parts of your spec directly: confirmation is mandatory, and momentum-failure exits are first-class, not optional.
A weak hint, not evidence: events where the chosen option's mid had already moved ≥+3% during the triggering 30 seconds had a somewhat better best move and a shallower 120-second dip in Mode A, but the sample is n = 6. That is enough to state a hypothesis to test, and nowhere near enough to set a threshold.

  5. Paper-trade integration only after the validation report shows the confirmed events beat the random baseline out-of-sample.

6. Method and limitations

  • Read-only SELECT queries against paper_decisions, option_chain_raw, hr_ohlc_5s, hr_option_5s, hr_instruments and hr_capture_sessions. Nothing was written.
  • Entry is always at the ask on the bar after the trigger, and exit is valued at the bid. LTP is never used as an execution price.
  • The 1-minute snapshots land ~0–30 s into the minute, and the 3-minute best/worst values do not show which came first.
  • The event threshold is leave-one-day-out over only 3 other days, so it is itself noisy. It diagnoses; it does not propose.
  • Hindsight labels in §2 measure whether an opportunity existed, not whether it was detectable.
  • No slippage beyond the quoted spread is modelled; the ATM strike is fixed at the 15:00 (1-min) or 14:54 (HR) reference.
