The one real defect: zero IV and zero Greeks on deep-ITM puts. Broken down by ATM offset, the zeros are entirely confined to strikes above spot on the PE side:

2026-09-03 SENSEX — +2PE 6/46, +3PE 36/46, +4PE 43/46, +5PE 42/46.
2026-09-07 NIFTY — +2PE 28/46, +3PE 46/46, +4PE 46/46, +5PE 46/46.

Dhan does not publish IV/Greeks for these illiquid legs. No strike at or below spot is affected. The paper agent is untouched by this (its ₹600 premium ceiling excludes deep-ITM options anyway), but ITM-put Greeks research is not possible on this data, and no amount of reprocessing will change that — the values were never published.
A labelling artefact worth knowing about. pipeline/catch_up_today.py stamps its single option-chain fetch with datetime.now(IST) — the wall-clock catch-up time, not a checkpoint time. That is a deliberate choice (its own code comment explains that reusing one fetch across ~375 checkpoints would falsely imply OI was polled at each past minute), but it means 2026-08-18 carries rows timestamped 16:27, after the close. Because get_option_chain_raw_near(at_or_before=...) filters on time, such a row can never be returned for a 14:59 query, so it cannot leak into a decision. It is a labelling quirk, not corruption — but a researcher filtering by date alone would need to know.

10. Gaps

  1. Sample size, not data quality, is the binding constraint. ~24–25 clean CAS-era days per symbol. This is the single biggest limitation on everything in §9, and it resolves only with time.
  2. ITM-put IV and Greeks are absent — broker-side, not recoverable by reprocessing.
  3. Two CAS-era days have no window data at all — 2026-08-18 and 2026-08-26.
  4. Intraday OI delta requires snapshot diffing; the stored field uses Dhan's prior-day-close baseline.
  5. No retry on a failed fetch — the minute is simply lost. This is the source of the 540-second gaps on 09-02 and 09-07.
  6. catch_up_today timestamps with wall-clock time, producing post-close fetched_at values (§6).

REASON: that is exactly what the platform already does, and this audit found no evidence for changing it. The API cost is 10% of Dhan's rate limit, storage is ~3.9 GB/year, and the CAS window is already 100% complete. Every narrower or coarser alternative trades away capability to conserve a resource that is not scarce.

Is full-day 1-minute capture genuinely necessary?

For the 14:59 CAS decision alone, no — Option D would be sufficient. For the Part J research programme, yes. Accumulation, liquidity sweeps, structural breaks and displacement are all pre-event phenomena: asking at 15:00 what option positioning looked like at 11:00 is only answerable if 11:00 was captured.

The decisive argument is asymmetry, not storage. Dhan has no historical option-chain API. A minute that is not captured is gone permanently — it cannot be backfilled at any price. Narrowing the window is therefore an irreversible decision made on incomplete information about what future research will need, while keeping it costs ~3.9 GB/year and 10% of a rate limit. That asymmetry settles the question on its own.

13. Is any change required now?

No. The capture system already implements the recommended policy. Two minor improvements are worth considering later — a bounded retry on a failed fetch (which would have closed the 540-second gaps on 09-02 and 09-07), and using the checkpoint time rather than wall-clock time in the catch-up path. Neither is urgent, neither affects the CAS window, and both are explicitly out of scope while the experiment is frozen.
